# Version 0.9.1 — 16 November 2026

This maintenance release aligns the package and both user guides with the manuscript dated November 16, 2026. Its exact build is `0.9.1-20261116`. Cohort-wise Wald-BIC selection already used joint MD for the final estimates; that estimator and the existing selection and inference engines are retained.

The November 16, 2026 terminology updates are documentation-only and retain version 0.9.1 and its build identifier. The guides and package documentation use asymptotic mean squared error (AMSE), MSE, or RMSE for estimation accuracy, as appropriate, and rejection probability for McCloskey calibration. They use standard errors, explain when selected restrictions are treated as fixed, and replace estimation-action terminology with estimator or procedure. They also explain the centered Gaussian bootstrap that repeats selection and estimation, covariate standardization, and the numerical error controls needed for conservative probability bounds. Estimation and inference code, command syntax, and stored diagnostic identifiers are unchanged. In particular, `risk_upper` and `risk_target` retain their existing names. No simulations or empirical results were rerun for these terminology updates.

- **Matched MD comparison:** `estat compare [, cells output(#)]` reports baseline, own-cohort MD, and joint MD estimates under identical selected restrictions, target weights, and sampling covariance. The own-cohort aggregate retains cross-cohort covariance. The command returns estimates, standard errors calculated treating the selected restrictions as fixed, point-estimate differences, covariance matrices, loadings, selected masks, and target maps. `cells` and `output()` are mutually exclusive. Scalar population sources are rejected because they do not supply disaggregated cohort effects. Active estimation results are preserved.
- **Focused comparisons:** the primary cohort-focused comparison uses the union of cohort selections, with its own-cohort slices for own MD. A separate aggregate-focused output uses its own selected joint mask. No reselection is performed. The comparison supplies descriptive diagnostics treating the selected restrictions as fixed and does not transfer existing inference to a new estimator or establish the extra cross-target assumptions.
- **Defaults and reproducibility:** documentation distinguishes nested candidates with a 4,096-candidate cap and ordinary WBIC normal intervals from the explicit all-subsets and centered-bootstrap workflow. `candidates(all) maxcand(16384)` accommodates up to 14 optional dates per cohort. The example keeps the target window identical through comparison and inference. The maintained baseline, anticipation, eligibility, covariance, and empirical target still require application-specific choices.
- **Inference language:** centered Gaussian reselection is described as a numerical approximation to a pointwise asymptotic construction, without a higher-order improvement claim. Fixed simulation budgets leave quantile error. The McCloskey `certified` compatibility label does not assert formal outward rounding of all calculations. Focused search remains a finite multistart numerical search, and independent simulation at discovered points does not establish global extrema. Cross-target focused inference additionally requires its stated limiting-tie regularity condition.
- **Synchronized documentation:** installed help, the quick guide, and the full guide describe the same release. The full guide preserves the legacy all-candidate envelope and cross-target extension as advanced material. Manuscript references use its date rather than an internal revision identifier.

See `VALIDATION.md` for executed release checks and the separation between current and historical evidence. This release does not rerun the manuscript's complete simulations or empirical archive, change its reported numerical results, or supply a new global numerical certification algorithm. Recreate prepared inference jobs after upgrading and verify the installed path using `which jwselect`.

## Historical release: version 0.9.0 — 4 November 2026

This release builds on the 0.8.9 implementation. It is the definitive 0.9.0 release, replacing the 0.10.0 name supplied during revision. A distinct build marker, `0.9.0-20261104`, prevents collision with an archived older local development branch that also used 0.9.0; version renumbering does not change numerical estimators. The manuscript relaxes ETWFE moment conditions and extends the RCS treatment of changing composition, while the software supplies explicit supported data and covariance contracts.

- **Nonlinear DiD:** `jwselect` after supported `jwdid` logit or Poisson fits now defaults to `restrictions(index)`, retaining the established full conditional-index formulation. Each optional date contributes its complete identified intercept-and-covariate block, with a rank penalty and full joint ATT/block covariance. Explicit `restrictions(placebo)` retains the legacy average fitted-placebo option as a distinct moment choice; it is not equivalent to the conditional-index null. A weaker target-specific average formulation is only a possible further extension requiring its own identifying map, joint estimation and covariance argument. It is not implemented, and there is no public `restrictions(average)` option.
- **Panel DR:** the `csdid` adapter requires `pscoretrim(1)`. The default `.995` and all other cutoffs below one are rejected, even if no observation happened to be trimmed in a particular sample. The analytical, unweighted balanced-panel, never-treated, universal-baseline contract remains in force.
- **Repeated-cross-section DR:** new `jwselect_rcs` estimates a target-specific ATT by standardizing each cohort-date outcome mean to the treated group's postperiod covariate distribution. Composition may change. The complete influence function includes all nuisance, target-distribution and ratio-normalization terms. `sampling(pooled)` is the default; `sampling(stratified)` conditions on fixed independent date sample sizes. The implementation uses finite-dimensional OLS/logit nuisances, no clipping or trimming, and one prespecified target per source fit. Representative independent samples, overlap, treatment-unaffected covariates and the target-standardized identifying restriction remain necessary. No anticipation must hold under the same target standardization for each included treated preperiod; conditional no anticipation in its source cell is sufficient, whereas a zero source-population mean alone need not suffice.
- **Sun–Abraham population IW:** new `jwselect_sa_iw` computes a prespecified event-window target and its joint covariance with optional cohort preperiod contrasts, including estimated cohort-share uncertainty. It supports the same balanced, unweighted, no-covariate, never-treated design as `jwselect_sa`. The existing direct route retains fixed reporting weights.
- **Scalar target selection:** the new RCS and population-IW sources enter manual, WBIC and aggregate-focused MD as one target with a complete joint covariance. They do not average separately selected cohort estimates or report artificial cohort effects. Source data signatures and design maps are validated; target design changes require a new source fit.
- **ETWFE regularity:** the manuscript replaces the joint raw-moment envelope with conditions on centered score products and derivative products, with separate uniform-integrability conditions for the uniform extension. This is a theoretical relaxation, not a new ETWFE estimator.

## Validation and historical material

The release includes targeted checks for nonlinear full-block covariance and default dispatch, composition-changing RCS transport and DR branches, pooled/stratified covariance, scalar source validation, actual untrimmed `csdid` integration, and SA share-variance contributions and downstream selection. The test files and their logs record which checks were executed. They are implementation checks, not empirical proof of the identifying assumptions.

The manuscript's historical Monte Carlo experiments, application tables and numerical interval comparisons have **not** been regenerated under this release. They must not be read as validation of the new defaults or source routes. Both bundled PDF user guides have been revised for this release; their editable LaTeX sources and the installed help files describe the same current interfaces.

The existing inference engines retain their documented limitations. In particular, the McCloskey calculation uses double precision and numerical padding, without a formal interval-arithmetic certificate; the numerical focused search does not certify global extrema. These revisions do not claim to resolve those separate numerical issues.
